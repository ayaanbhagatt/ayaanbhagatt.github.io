# backpack design process

For this unit, I focused on one of my friends issues of their backpack getting uncomfortable to carry throughout the school day. I interviewwed them & came up with different ideas then further developed three of them, and finally I chose one to experiment with.

## interview

I talked to my friend Brandon about his life and his issues carrying his backpack around school. He usually has textbooks, a laptop, notebooks, and sometimes sports stuff. Some days are heavier depending on his classes, so his shoulders get sore after carrying it for a while, especially toward the end of the day or when walking farther around campus.

One shoulder sometimes feels worse than the other. He usually uses both straps, but sometimes only one, and adjusting them hasn't really helped. We also talked about packing. His laptop and heavier books are usually toward the back, but things can shift around. He doesn't really know how much the backpack weighs.

What stood out to me was that he flat out does not want to carry fewer things, since most are needed for class. I couldn't assume the issue was just total weight. Uneven weight distribution seemed worth looking into as well.

## main principles

From the interview, these were the main things I wanted to keep in mind, I guess there were others but this stuff was the most important:

- Reduce pressure on shoulders especially when one side is taking more of the load.
- Keep things he needs available when he needs them.
- Account for classes and different amounts of stuff each day.
- Make it work while walking around campus(portability)
- Keep it simple enough that using it doesn't make his day more annoying (convinience) 

The space I focused on was how to make carrying materials more comfortable either by distributing the load differently or changing when he has to carry certain items.

## raw ideas(sorry in advance)

I tried to get different ideas down before deciding which ones were actually realistic. Some were pretty out there(bad), but they helped me think beyond just making a lighter backpack.

1. Pressure sensors on both shoulders connected to a system that transfers load when one side has more pressure.
2. A backpack with hidden straps connecting to other parts of the body to spread out the weight.
3. Inflatable shoulder pads that adjust based on pressure to distribute the force.
4. A backpack drone that follows you and supports part of the weight.
5. Put a lot of balloons or inflatables on the backpack to help lift it.
6. A body covering exoskeleton with compartments for carrying things.
7. A magnetic backpack so you can lean against walls and take some of the load off.
8. Pressurized air thrusters to help support the backpack.
9. A hopping backpack robot that carries your things and follows you.
10. Outsource carrying things to other people's backpacks for a small fee.

## more complete ideas

I developed three of these into gallery sketches.

### 1 - hidden straps

This idea uses strong, adjustable straps that go from the backpack under clothing and spread the load to other parts of the body. Organizers inside would help keep the weight balanced. The goal would be to take some load off the shoulders. I would still need to figure out whether the extra straps are comfortable and easy to put on.

[![Gallery sketch 1: hidden straps that distribute backpack weight across the body]({{ '/backpack-hidden-straps.jpg' | relative_url }})]({{ '/backpack-hidden-straps.jpg' | relative_url }})

### 2 - pressure sensors and a hydraulic system

This idea puts pressure sensors on both shoulder straps. A connected hydraulic system would try to equalize the pressure if one side is carrying more. It wouldn't remove the total load but it could make it less uneven. It would need reliable sensors, flexible materials, and as little added weight as possible(more would definitely be an issue). This one would take a LOT of work to build and test.

[![Gallery sketch 2: shoulder pressure sensors connected to a hydraulic balancing system]({{ '/backpack-pressure-sensors.jpg' | relative_url }})]({{ '/backpack-pressure-sensors.jpg' | relative_url }})

### 3 - use space in other people's backpacks(my favorite and his as well)

Someone else carries items that aren't needed until a certain class or activity. "Handoffs" of items would wrap around already overlapping schedules if that makes sense. My page includes a small fee based on the item's weight or how fragile it is, and a plan for forgotten items. It would depend a lot on people being reliable(hopefully).

[![Gallery sketch 3: sharing backpack space and arranging item handoffs around the school day]({{ '/backpack-sharing.jpg' | relative_url }})]({{ '/backpack-sharing.jpg' | relative_url }})

## experiment & path forward

After talking again, Brandon decided he wanted to try the third idea. I personally also want to test if having someone else carry a few items can make my his backpack easier to carry without making his day unnecessarily complicated. Its also pretty easy to try a small version of this before building anything. The current plan is to choose one or two heavier/fragile items that he only needs for a specific class or activity. For a single school day someone who goes to his school(he's not at Webb) would carry those items and meet him before he needs them. We would plan handoffs where their schedules already overlap so neither person has to make extra trips. On a normal day and the experiment day, I would ask him to rate how heavy the backpack feels and how comfortable it is to carry, both from 1 to 10. For heaviness, 1 would mean very light and 10 very heavy. For comfort, 1 would mean very uncomfortable and 10 very comfortable. I would ask at similar points in the day and try to compare days with similar classes and materials.

Afterward, I would ask whether the difference was noticeable, whether he worried about getting his things on time, and if parts of the handoffs were annoying. I would also ask the person helping whether carrying the extra items was manageable. The experiment would be successful if his backpack felt easier to carry and the handoffs fit naturally into both people's days. I obviously haven't run the experiment yet. However, after Thanksgiving break, I'll run it and use feedback to decide whether to keep developing it or scrap it entirely.

## reflections

I think the most useful part of the interview was learning why he keeps carrying so much and why having less is an issue.  It seems like an obvious answer but that doesn't really help if he needs those things for class. Asking about his routine gave me more to work with than just asking how the bag was heavy. I found it interesting that one shoulder could feel worse even when he usually uses both straps. That made me think alot more about weight distribution. The raw ideas let me put down things like drones and backpack robots without figuring out every detail immediately, I guess allowing my creativity to run wild. The gallery sketches kinda also made me think more about materials, reliability, and practicality of  each idea. After starting with so many hardware ideas it was pretty surprising that we chose something without any of those things As for the future I'll take with me the idea of understanding someone's routine before deciding the idea, and testing the main assumption early. Here I guess that question is whether carrying less is worth relying on someone else for your stuff(and we'll see!!!!)
